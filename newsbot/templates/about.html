How {{ site.title }} works

{{ site.tagline }}

{{ site.title }} is an experiment in automated journalism. Nobody edits this site by hand. A scheduled job runs every few hours and does the following:

  1. Reads the feeds. It downloads the public RSS and Atom feeds of {{ site.source_count }} feeds from news organisations on every continent.
  2. Finds the stories. Headlines that describe the same event are grouped together, so one story can draw on several outlets in several countries.
  3. Reads the originals. For the lead sources it fetches the article page (respecting each site's robots.txt) so the writer has more than a blurb to work from.
  4. Writes. A language model writes an original article from those reports, with a strict instruction to use only facts that appear in the sources, to attribute them, and to say when sources disagree. Each draft is checked for copied wording and rejected if it echoes the sources too closely. A story that cannot be written safely is skipped, never copied.
  5. Compares. Where the reports give different figures or accounts, the article has a "Where the reports differ" box that names the outlets.
  6. Illustrates. Photos come from Wikimedia Commons under open licences, show a place or subject related to the story rather than the event, are captioned as illustrative, and are credited on the credits page.
  7. Publishes. The article is saved with links to every source it used, and the site, its RSS feeds and the daily briefing are rebuilt.

What this means for you

The articles here are machine-written summaries of other people's reporting. They can contain mistakes, and they will always lag the original coverage. Each article shows a confidence level (how well its sources agreed) and lists its sources; please read those before relying on anything you find here. Nothing on this site should be treated as original reporting.

For publishers

The pipeline only uses feeds that publishers make available for syndication, fetches each article page at most once, honours robots.txt, and never republishes full articles. Every story links back to its sources. If you would like a feed removed from the catalogue, open an issue on the project repository.
